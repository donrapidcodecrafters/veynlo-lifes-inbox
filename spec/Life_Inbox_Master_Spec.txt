Consumer Life Operating System • iOS • Android • Web • Windows • macOS


Product promise  Your life remembers itself. Life Inbox continuously captures, understands, organizes, verifies, and resurfaces the information that matters—so users do not have to manually maintain a second version of their lives inside an app.

Absolute product rules
Rule
Requirement
Evidence before assertion
Every material extracted fact must retain source provenance. The UI must expose “Why am I seeing this?” / “Where did this come from?” and confidence/verification status when useful.
No silent hallucination
Unknown dates, amounts, identities, relationships, or obligations remain unknown. The system may create a candidate for review; it may not invent a precise value to appear helpful.
Authorization before retrieval
Household privacy and object ACLs are enforced before search/AI retrieval. A language model must never receive data the requesting principal is not authorized to see.
High-risk confirmation
Destructive, financial, legal, healthcare, identity, account-security, or irreversible actions require explicit confirmation unless the user has created a narrow, comprehensible autopilot rule that is itself permitted.
User control
Connections can be paused or disconnected. Data can be corrected, exported, deleted by scope, or fully deleted. Automation can be disabled. Notifications can be tuned.
No privacy theater
No selling private life content, no behavioral advertising based on private content, no training general-purpose models on private user content without explicit opt-in and clear separation.
Graceful degradation
A failed connector, expired token, provider outage, missing webhook, model outage, or offline device must not silently create a false “all caught up” state.
Cross-platform parity with native advantages
Core information and actions are available everywhere; platform-specific integrations such as Apple EventKit, share extensions, widgets, and biometrics are used where they produce a better experience.

1. Product Vision, Outcome & Positioning
Life Inbox is a consumer life operating system. It does not ask people to become diligent database administrators of their own lives. It connects to the systems they already use, accepts anything they intentionally share, converts unstructured signals into verified structured knowledge, and continuously decides what deserves attention now, later, or never.
North-star experience  A new user connects one source and, within the first session, Life Inbox finds useful things they did not manually enter: an upcoming obligation, a return deadline, an appointment, a delivery, a recurring charge, or a document expiration. The product earns permission to expand only after demonstrating value.

1.1 Product outcome hierarchy
Level
Outcome
Examples
Remember
Preserve information the user would otherwise lose or have to search for.
Receipt, serial number, reservation, saved restaurant, contractor name, warranty email.
Understand
Convert raw information into structured facts and relationships.
“This email is a purchase; this attachment is the receipt; this date is the return deadline.”
Protect
Prevent missed deadlines, lost money, forgotten renewals, duplicated obligations, and unsafe blind spots.
Passport expiration, subscription price increase, product recall, expiring insurance, missed return window.
Coordinate
Resolve conflicts between people, calendars, obligations, places, and resources.
Two children need rides at the same time; hotel date disagrees with calendar; household bill assigned to wrong person.
Act
Prepare or perform safe actions with explicit user control.
Add event, file receipt, draft cancellation, create reminder, reorder filter, prepare registration renewal.
Compound
Become more useful over years because history, ownership, relationships, preferences, and verified facts accumulate.
“Who repaired the furnace in 2028?” “What did we pay?” “Is it still under warranty?”

1.3 Positioning
• Not a to-do list: tasks may be created, but most valuable obligations are discovered automatically.
• Not a calendar replacement: it understands calendars plus the information that never made it onto the calendar.
• Not a budgeting app: it can understand money, recurring obligations, and purchases without requiring envelope budgeting as the primary experience.
• Not a cloud drive: it stores important evidence and documents but organizes them around life objects and actions instead of folders alone.
• Not merely an AI chatbot: the conversational layer is an interface over structured, permissioned, evidence-backed personal data.
• Not an advertising profile: monetization is subscription-first. Commercial recommendations must be clearly labeled, user-beneficial, and isolated from private-content targeting.
2. Product Principles & Non-Goals
2.1 Product principles
Principle
Detailed product requirement
Zero-entry bias
Prefer automatic discovery, share-to-capture, scanning, and inference over forcing users to fill forms. Manual entry remains available for corrections and unsupported sources.
Progressive trust
Ask for the smallest permission capable of showing value. Explain why a permission helps before invoking the OS/provider consent sheet. Never bundle unrelated scopes merely because they might be useful someday.
Evidence is part of the UI
A fact is not only a value. Users must be able to inspect source email/document/transaction, extracted passage, timestamp, and verification state where material.
Attention is scarce
The product succeeds by suppressing noise as much as by finding information. Low-value discoveries belong in digests or search, not constant push notifications.
Users own corrections
A direct user assertion outranks a model inference unless later evidence clearly conflicts; conflicts must be surfaced rather than silently overwriting important user-confirmed data.
One life object, many signals
Multiple source events referring to the same purchase, vehicle, subscription, trip, person, or appliance should converge on a single object and history.
Safe automation is reversible
Automated mutations should be logged, explainable, and undoable or compensatable whenever the external service permits.
Platform honest
Do not imply background access to iMessage, WhatsApp personal chats, banking, retailer accounts, or smart-home systems when no supported permission/API exists. Use share sheet, forwarding, email inference, file import, or partnerships as appropriate.
Longevity
Schemas and event history are versioned. The product must survive model changes, connector migrations, device changes, and a decade of user history.

2.2 Explicit non-goals for initial releases
• No autonomous medical diagnosis, treatment advice, or clinical record system. Health functionality is logistical and document/reminder oriented until separate regulatory, clinical-safety, and compliance programs exist.
• No full autonomous banking or investment management. Financial connections first provide awareness, categorization, reminders, and prepared actions; money movement requires separate regulated partnerships and risk controls.
• No promise to read private messaging systems that do not offer consumer APIs. Shared content can still be captured explicitly.
• No attempt to replace every specialized app in v1. Life Inbox is the connective personal intelligence layer; native replacement functionality should be added only where it reduces meaningful friction.
• No behavioral advertising marketplace built from private user content.

HH-001 — Household creation and membership
Purpose
Allow people to coordinate shared life data without collapsing personal privacy boundaries.
User experience / visible behavior
A user may create a named household, invite adults by email/link, add dependent profiles, and see membership status. Shared areas are visually distinct from private areas.
User actions
Create, rename, invite, resend, revoke, accept/decline, leave, remove, transfer household ownership, set member label, add dependent.
Backend behavior
Membership service issues signed invitations, stores role and lifecycle state, and recomputes effective grants. Removal revokes future access immediately and triggers device/cache invalidation.
Primary data / state
households, household_memberships, invitations, dependent_profiles, resource_grants, audit_events.
Permissions / consent
Adult invites require recipient acceptance. Sensitive shared categories may require explicit per-category opt-in.
Failure, ambiguity & edge states
Expired invite, duplicate invite, already-member email, owner attempts to leave without transfer, billing owner removal, dependent aging into own account, household merge/split.
Analytics / quality signals
Invite acceptance rate, time to first shared item, membership churn, unauthorized-access denials, support incidents.

4.2 Shared code strategy
• Monorepo with separate applications for web, mobile, desktop, extension, and internal admin; shared packages for domain types, API client, validation schemas, feature flags, analytics events, design tokens, formatting, and authorization-aware presentation rules.
• Native modules are preferred over brittle cross-platform workarounds when an OS capability is central to the product. The domain contract stays shared even if capture/permission code differs.
• Every server-visible object uses stable IDs and version metadata so the same account can move between devices without semantic drift.
• Platform differences must be documented in a capability registry that the UI can inspect. A feature hidden by provider/OS limitation should explain the limitation rather than failing mysteriously.

5. Account, Authentication & Onboarding
AUTH-001 — Secure account identity
Purpose
Provide low-friction sign-in with modern phishing-resistant options and reliable recovery.
User experience / visible behavior
Welcome supports Sign in with Apple, Google, Microsoft, passkey, and email-based account flow. Existing-account detection prevents accidental duplicate accounts. Device list and session activity are visible in settings.
User actions
Create account, sign in, add/remove sign-in method, create passkey, reauthenticate, revoke device, sign out everywhere, recover account.
Backend behavior
Identity service maps multiple identity-provider subjects to one internal user. Sessions are short-lived/access + rotating refresh tokens or provider-backed equivalent. Risk signals can trigger step-up authentication.
Primary data / state
users, identities, sessions, devices, passkeys metadata, recovery state, audit_events.
Permissions / consent
Provider OAuth/OIDC scopes are separate from data connector scopes. Account recovery changes require strong verification.
Failure, ambiguity & edge states
Lost email access, Apple private relay address, duplicate provider identities, device theft, passkey unavailable, provider outage, account linking conflict.
Analytics / quality signals
Sign-in success, recovery success, duplicate-account incidents, step-up rate, compromised-session signals.
Entitlement
Core

AUTH-002 — Device trust and biometric protection
Purpose
Protect sensitive local and server data without requiring constant passwords.
User experience / visible behavior
Users can enable Face ID/Touch ID/Android biometrics/app lock; settings list trusted devices with last activity and approximate location/device type. Sensitive vault views may require reauthentication.
User actions
Enable/disable app lock, mark device trusted, revoke device, require reauth for categories, copy/reveal sensitive fields.
Backend behavior
Device registration issues per-device key material; sensitive local cache is encrypted. Server authorization can require recent authentication for high-risk endpoints.
Primary data / state
devices, device_keys metadata, auth_assurance, security_preferences.
Permissions / consent
Biometric data remains OS-managed; application stores only authorization outcome/key wrappers, never biometric templates.
Failure, ambiguity & edge states
Biometric enrollment changes, device restore, rooted/jailbroken risk policy, desktop without biometric hardware, offline reauth.
Analytics / quality signals
App-lock adoption, failed unlocks, revoked-device access attempts.
Entitlement
Plus+

ONB-001 — Value-first onboarding
Purpose
Prove value before asking users to architect their life.
User experience / visible behavior
Onboarding asks what the user most wants help with—“important dates,” “purchases & returns,” “bills & subscriptions,” “family,” “travel,” “things I own”—then recommends one useful source. After consent, a bounded initial scan produces an immediate discovery summary.
User actions
Choose goal, connect source, choose history depth, review discoveries, confirm/correct, skip optional setup, invite household later.
Backend behavior
Onboarding orchestration creates an import job, tracks provider sync, runs relevance/classification/extraction, generates candidate objects, and returns progress in resumable stages.
Primary data / state
onboarding_state, connection, import_job, candidates, consent_receipts, feature_preferences.
Permissions / consent
Pre-permission screen must explain exact reason and category. Do not ask for unrelated scopes.
Failure, ambiguity & edge states
User abandons OAuth, zero relevant discoveries, enormous mailbox, provider throttling, scan interrupted, permission partially granted.
Analytics / quality signals
Connect conversion, time-to-first-value, discoveries/user, confirmation rate, onboarding completion, D1/D7 retention.
Entitlement
Core

ONB-002 — Historical depth control
Purpose
Let users trade completeness, time, privacy, and processing cost intentionally.
User experience / visible behavior
Before a large import the user chooses Forward only, 30 days, 90 days, 6 months, 1 year, or “build my history” where provider capability allows. Estimated processing volume/cost restrictions may be explained by plan.
User actions
Select/change depth, pause/resume backfill, request deeper backfill later.
Backend behavior
Connector creates paginated backfill with checkpoint cursor; deduplication makes repeated ranges idempotent. Processing budgets, rate limits, and provider quotas are enforced.
Primary data / state
connection_sync_state, backfill_ranges, cursors, processing_budget.
Permissions / consent
Historical scan scope is disclosed. Deeper scan may require additional provider permission depending connector.
Failure, ambiguity & edge states
Provider history unavailable, deleted source item, mailbox too large, user downgrades during backfill.
Analytics / quality signals
Backfill completion, cost/imported relevant item, processing errors, depth upgrades.

8. Universal Inbox
INB-001 — Discovery inbox
Purpose
Give newly discovered information a temporary review space before it disappears into structured life data.
User experience / visible behavior
Cards show category, concise extracted meaning, source, confidence, related object, proposed action, and whether the system has already auto-filed it. Filters include Needs Review, Auto-filed, Duplicates, Low Confidence, and category.
User actions
Confirm, correct, merge, split, file, archive, dismiss, block sender/type, create rule, inspect source.
Backend behavior
Candidate service stores extraction output separately from canonical facts until auto-accept threshold/policy permits promotion. User actions generate training/evaluation labels without overwriting source history.
Primary data / state
candidate_entities, candidate_facts, source_events, review_actions.
Permissions / consent
Review list is ACL-filtered; sensitive previews can be masked.
Failure, ambiguity & edge states
Repeated marketing email, source deleted after capture, conflicting candidate with user-confirmed fact, parser regression.
Analytics / quality signals
Review burden/user, auto-file precision, correction rate, category-specific false positives.

Failure, ambiguity & edge states

9. Ask Life Inbox & Search
ASK-001 — Universal natural-language ask
Purpose
Let users retrieve, compare, summarize, and act on their verified life data conversationally.
User experience / visible behavior
Ask screen accepts text/voice. Answers lead with concise result, then supporting cards/sources, uncertainty if any, and safe suggested actions. It can answer “When does my refrigerator warranty expire?” or “What hotel did we stay at in Denver?”
User actions
Ask, refine, filter, open source, correct answer, save query, create reminder/rule from result, share authorized result.
Backend behavior
Query orchestrator classifies intent, applies authorization filters before retrieval, combines structured SQL/search with semantic retrieval, reranks, synthesizes answer, and attaches evidence IDs.
Primary data / state
query_session, retrieval_log, answer_evidence, user_feedback.
Permissions / consent
No retrieval of inaccessible household items. Sensitive categories may require recent reauth.
Failure, ambiguity & edge states
Ambiguous person/object, insufficient evidence, contradictory sources, stale source, adversarial document content, unsupported action.
Analytics / quality signals
Answer acceptance, source-open rate, correction rate, unsupported-query rate, latency, cost/query.
Entitlement
Core with usage limits; Plus+ expanded

ASK-002 — Structured search and filters
Purpose
Support deterministic retrieval without requiring conversation.
User experience / visible behavior
Search offers instant suggestions across people, objects, documents, dates, merchants, places, tags, and exact identifiers. Filter chips narrow source, category, household member, date, verified state, amount, and status.
User actions
Search, filter, sort, save view, export results where allowed.
Backend behavior
Hybrid index combines normalized fields, full text, aliases, OCR text, and embeddings. Search documents are security-scoped and revalidated at fetch time.
Primary data / state
search_documents, embeddings, entity_aliases, ACL metadata.
Permissions / consent
Never rely solely on cached index ACLs; result fetch rechecks authorization.
Failure, ambiguity & edge states
Recently revoked share still indexed, typo, multilingual query, OCR errors, deleted source.
Analytics / quality signals
Search success, zero-results, reformulation, click-through, stale-index defects.
Entitlement
Core

ASK-003 — Vague-memory retrieval
Purpose

30. Location & Context
30.1 Context principles
Location must be opt-in, purpose-specific, battery-aware, and easy to disable. The product should prefer OS geofencing/significant-change mechanisms over continuous background tracking. Users must be able to use Life Inbox fully without granting location.

Purpose
Store home, work, school, doctor, retailer, airport, favorite places or any saved location.
User experience / visible behavior
Place detail links relevant people, trips, saved items, tasks and history.
User actions

Backend behavior
Location service registers OS-native geofence/context trigger IDs on device; server stores rule geometry minimally. Travel-time queries are purpose-bound; event logs avoid unnecessary raw coordinates.
Primary data / state
places, geofences, context_rules, location_permission_state, travel_estimates.
Permissions / consent
Separate foreground/background/precise consent; clear explanation; no location required for core.

34. Rules, Automation & Agent Actions
34.1 Automation risk levels
Level
Examples
Default policy
L0 — Organize
Tag, classify, link evidence, archive low-value item.
May run automatically if reversible and high confidence.
L1 — Personal local action
Create Life Inbox reminder, add internal note, schedule internal maintenance.
Can auto-run under user preference; always logged/undoable.
L2 — External low-risk mutation
Add/update calendar event, create external task, file cloud copy.
Explicit rule or per-action confirmation; connector write scope required.
L3 — Consequential communication/commerce
Send message, cancel subscription, place order, book appointment, submit form.
Prepare by default; explicit confirmation immediately before external action unless narrow approved automation with provider safeguards.
L4 — High-risk/irreversible
Money movement, legal acceptance/signature, identity renewal submission, unlock/security control, healthcare action.
Separate product controls, recent strong authentication, explicit confirmation; some actions remain out of scope entirely.

39.2 Model routing, versioning and evaluation
• Maintain a model registry containing provider, model/version, supported tasks, max context, structured-output reliability, latency class, cost class, regions, privacy/retention settings, launch status, and deprecation date.
• Every prompt/schema/extractor has an immutable version. Persist the extractor/model version that created each machine-derived fact so the system can explain or reprocess historical data.
• Validate every structured model response against a schema; invalid output retries through a constrained repair path or alternate model, never silently enters canonical data.
• Per-domain offline evaluation suites include precision/recall of fields, date/amount exactness, entity-linking precision, false critical alert rate, and calibration by confidence band.
• Production canaries compare new model output on sampled, privacy-controlled or synthetic/redacted inputs before changing canonical extraction behavior.
• Global kill switch must disable a model or task without client release; fallback routing can use deterministic-only/degraded behavior if all models are unavailable.
• Budget guardrails exist per user, plan, source type, historical backfill, Ask request, and agent workflow. Defer low-priority historical enrichment before degrading critical current processing.

40.2 Merge, unmerge and identity history
• Canonical entities retain immutable internal IDs. A merge does not destroy component identity: merge lineage stores source IDs, reason, algorithm/policy version, confidence, actor and timestamp.
• User can inspect and undo a merge. Unmerge rebuilds search indexes, timeline projections, relationships and domain projections idempotently.
• Automatic entity merge uses precision-first thresholds; false non-merge is preferable to incorrectly combining two people, accounts, vehicles, purchases or medical/identity items.
• Provider deletes/renames do not break canonical identity; provider aliases and source identities are historized.
• Conflicting user-confirmed identities are never silently resolved by AI. Present a comparison with evidence and resulting impact before merge.

42. API, Events, Jobs & Offline Sync
42.1 API contract requirements
• Use opaque typed resource IDs; never expose database sequence IDs as authorization assumptions.
• All retryable writes accept an idempotency key. External side effects require durable command identity independent of HTTP retry.
• Optimistic concurrency/version or ETag for mutable records. High-impact stale edits return a conflict, not last-write-wins.
• Cursor pagination for Inbox, timeline, events, documents and other large collections; stable sort key and snapshot semantics documented.
• Errors contain machine code, user-safe localized message, retryability, field-level details, and trace/reference ID. Provider raw errors are mapped and not leaked with secrets.
• API versioning supports additive change and a documented mobile minimum-version policy. Old clients can be remotely blocked only for security/data-integrity reasons with upgrade UX.
• Read models may aggregate across modules for Home and object detail; write commands remain explicit and policy checked.

42.4 Event contract rules
• Events describe something that happened; commands request something to happen. Never overload one type for both.
• Each event includes event_id, type/version, occurred_at, recorded_at, actor/principal, tenant/owner scope, aggregate/resource ID, correlation/causation IDs, sensitivity metadata, and payload.
• Consumers are idempotent. Exactly-once business effects are achieved through idempotency/transactional outbox/inbox patterns, not assumptions about message-broker delivery.
• Payloads avoid embedding entire raw emails/documents. Reference canonical resources/evidence instead to reduce leakage and event-schema bloat.
• Schema compatibility and migration strategy are documented; old consumers can ignore additive fields.

42.6 Offline sync and conflict model
• Encrypted local store caches Home, upcoming events/tasks, recently accessed objects, selected offline documents/trip/emergency packet, preferences, and pending mutations.
• Clients maintain server version and incremental sync token. Offline command receives a client-generated command/idempotency ID and remains visibly Pending until server confirms.
• Use field/type-specific conflict policy: display preferences may use last-write-wins; freeform notes need merge/revision handling; provider calendar edits honor provider ETags; high-impact dates/amounts require review when conflicting.
• Deletions create tombstones long enough to prevent stale devices from resurrecting data.
• Local caches are invalidated immediately on logout/session revoke/household access revoke when device is online; next sync enforces revocation and encrypted cache key rotation as appropriate.
• Offline Ask is limited to cached structured data unless a future local model/index is explicitly supported; UI states limitations honestly.

44.3 Storage policy
• PostgreSQL is canonical transactional storage. Avoid putting critical domain state only in vector/search indexes or analytics stores.
• Object storage holds original/derived file blobs; metadata in database. Content-addressed dedupe may avoid duplicate physical blobs while preserving per-user logical ownership and deletion accounting.
• Redis-compatible cache is ephemeral: feed fragments, short-lived query cache, rate limits, locks/coordination where safe; never canonical user content.
• Vector embeddings and search documents contain resource/tenant/sensitivity identifiers and must be deleted/reindexed with canonical data. Retrieval authorization is enforced before exposing results.
• Analytics warehouse receives minimized/pseudonymized product events. Raw email/document/financial/health/identity content is not copied into general analytics.
• Data retention supports “source + extracted,” “extracted only where feasible,” connection-specific deletion on disconnect, user-selected history limits, and legally/security-required audit retention.

45. Security, Privacy, Consent & Threat Model
45.1 Security baseline
• TLS for all network traffic; current secure protocol/cipher configuration and automated certificate management.
• Encryption at rest for database, object storage, backups and local caches; application-level encryption for highest-sensitivity identifiers and connector credentials where appropriate.
• Cloud KMS/HSM-backed key hierarchy, rotation, environment separation and least-privilege decrypt permissions.
• OAuth/provider tokens live in dedicated encrypted credential subsystem or secrets storage, referenced by ID from normal connection records; never log raw tokens.
• Passkeys and MFA supported; sessions are short-lived/rotated and independently revocable; sensitive settings/actions require recent authentication.
• Service-to-service identity uses workload identity/short-lived credentials; no long-lived cloud access keys embedded in code/images.
• SAST, dependency/SBOM, secret scanning, container/image scanning, IaC scanning, DAST/API tests, patch SLAs and regular third-party penetration testing.
• WAF/rate limits/bot protection for auth and high-abuse endpoints; malware scanning and file-type validation before content processing.
• Production access is just-in-time, role-scoped and audited. Support tools hide private content by default.

47.3 Reliability objectives
• Define SLOs separately for authentication, core read/write API, Home feed, critical notification timeliness, connector freshness by source class, search/Ask availability, billing entitlement propagation, export/deletion, and durable command execution.
• Provider integrations cannot all share one “uptime” metric. A provider outage should not make Life Inbox unavailable; existing cached/canonical data remains usable and affected freshness is disclosed.
• Every critical alert candidate stores when the underlying fact became known and when notification was sent so deadline-timeliness SLO is measurable.
• Recovery point and recovery time objectives are documented per data class; restore drills validate them.
47.4 AI/infrastructure unit-cost controls
• Track cost per active user, paid user, connected email account, thousand messages, document page, financial connection, Ask query, automation run and historical backfill.
• Known-template deterministic parsers bypass AI at high confidence. Relevance classifier must run before expensive extraction.
• Low-priority historical imports are batched and can pause under global/model/provider budget pressure; current critical sources are prioritized.
• Cache template/merchant classifications and reusable non-private metadata where safe. Do not cache cross-user private prompts/responses in ways that leak data.
• Use model-size tiers. Larger reasoning models are not the default receipt parser.
• Storage lifecycle deletes intermediate OCR artifacts/thumbnails no longer needed while preserving user-selected originals/canonical text according to policy.
• Gross margin by plan is a release gate for expensive new features. A feature that users love but costs more than its plan revenue needs pricing, quotas or architectural improvement before broad rollout.

49. DevOps, Deployment, Backup & Disaster Recovery
49.1 Environment and CI/CD requirements
• Separate local/dev/test/staging/production environments. Production private data is never copied into lower environments; use synthetic/anonymized fixtures.
• Infrastructure as code with peer review, policy checks and drift detection.
• CI runs formatting/lint, type checking, unit tests, integration/contract tests, migration tests, dependency/secret/SAST/IaC scans, build signing and artifact provenance.
• Deployment uses canary/rolling/blue-green patterns with health gates and rollback. Database migrations follow expand-migrate-contract and remain compatible with mixed mobile/server versions.
• Mobile and desktop releases are decoupled from server feature exposure through remote feature flags and minimum-version rules. Staged App Store/Play rollouts and desktop update rings reduce blast radius.
• Production access uses just-in-time audited roles and separate break-glass process.
49.2 Backup and recovery
Data/system
Backup / recovery requirement
PostgreSQL
Encrypted automated snapshots + point-in-time recovery; restore tested on schedule; backup account/keys separated where possible.
Object storage
Versioning/replication/lifecycle appropriate to region and threat model; deletion behavior documented relative to backups.
Queues/events
Durable broker/workflow persistence; business events/commands replayable without duplicate side effects.
Search/vector
Rebuildable from canonical data; backup optional based on recovery-time need, but authorization/deletion must remain consistent.
Configuration/secrets
IaC and configuration versioned; secrets backed by managed secrets/KMS with recovery procedure, never source control.
Mobile/desktop offline
Server is primary recovery source; selected offline-only unsynced captures queue until online and surface risk if app/device data is cleared.

49.3 Disaster recovery and integrity
• Document RPO/RTO by subsystem and run scheduled restore/failover exercises.
• Provider outages degrade connector freshness, not entire product. Existing canonical/cached information remains readable.
• Data-integrity jobs detect orphan evidence, missing blobs, broken relationships, duplicate commands, stale projections and index drift.
• Have emergency read-only mode if write correctness cannot be guaranteed during incident.
• Cross-region active/passive or active/active is added when business/regulatory needs justify and is not claimed until regularly tested.

51. App Store, Legal, Compliance & Governance Readiness
51.1 Platform distribution requirements
• Apple and Google subscription flows include accurate price/cadence, restore purchase, manage/cancel path, privacy disclosures and trial terms.
• Review Sign in with Apple requirements if other third-party login providers are offered on Apple platforms.
• Permission purpose text is specific and contextual for calendar, contacts, photos/files, notifications, microphone/camera, location and local network/home capabilities.
• Background processing/location is used only within platform policy; marketing never claims invisible continuous access the OS does not provide.
• Desktop apps are code-signed/notarized; update channel is signed and protected from downgrade/tamper.
• Browser extensions request the minimum host/active-tab permissions and explain captured data in store listing/privacy policy.
51.2 Governance roadmap
• Maintain a living data inventory, processing purpose, retention schedule, subprocessor register, regional storage/transfer mapping and data-subject-right workflow.
• Consumer privacy law obligations vary by region. Legal counsel maps exact launch jurisdictions; architecture supports access/export/delete/correction/consent withdrawal regardless.
• Child/dependent features require guardian/age model and regional legal review. Avoid collecting child data beyond guardian-managed logistics until controls are established.
• Financial aggregators/payment processors reduce direct regulated scope but do not remove Life Inbox security, privacy, consumer-protection and financial-claims responsibilities.
• Health remains logistics-only until a separate regulated program reviews HIPAA or other health-data obligations, clinical safety, business associate relationships and regional law.
• Easy cancellation, honest trial/renewal, non-destructive downgrade and no dark patterns are product requirements, not only legal text.
51.3 Documents/policies required before public launch
• Terms of Service; Privacy Policy; subscription/auto-renew terms; Acceptable Use Policy; Security overview; subprocessor list; data retention/deletion policy; cookie/web tracking policy; accessibility statement; vulnerability disclosure/responsible disclosure policy; law-enforcement request policy; family/child data disclosures; partner data-processing terms; app-store privacy/data-safety disclosures; copyright/DMCA process where applicable to user-saved content.

54.2 Launch acceptance criteria
1. New user can create an account, understand requested permission and connect Gmail or Outlook without support.
2. Initial processing shows incremental useful discoveries rather than an empty dashboard; each material detected fact has inspectable evidence.
3. Home, Inbox, Search/Ask and Timeline remain consistent through edits, source updates, merge/unmerge, deletion and reconnect.
4. Critical dates/amounts never present as certain below configured domain threshold; conflicts are surfaced.
5. Authorization test suite demonstrates no unauthorized household/private data can leak through API, search/Ask, notifications, widgets, shares, exports, caches or admin tools.
6. Connector flows survive duplicate webhooks, webhook expiration, provider rate limit, outage, token expiry/reauth and reconciliation/cursor reset.
7. Deduplication/entity resolution meets precision targets for launch domains; user can undo merges/corrections.
8. Notification engine respects quiet hours/sensitivity, does not duplicate unchanged information and suppresses completed/stale items.
9. Data export/deletion workflows cover canonical DB, objects, credentials, search/vector indexes, caches/materialized projections and configured processors according to policy.
10. Free/trial/paid/upgrade/downgrade/cancel/payment-failure/refund entitlements reconcile correctly across web and app stores.
11. Accessibility critical journeys pass manual and automated review on launch platforms.
12. Security review/penetration testing has no launch-blocking critical/high findings; incident response and revoke/kill-switch runbooks tested.
13. No provider capability is marketed beyond what the shipped integration actually supports.
54.3 Engineering definition of done — every feature
• User story, purpose and non-goals documented.
• Front-end behavior defined for mobile/web/desktop or explicitly not applicable; empty/loading/partial/error/offline/permission-denied states included.
• Canonical data model/migration and source-of-truth ownership reviewed.
• Sensitivity classification, authorization and household-sharing behavior reviewed.
• Connector/provider feasibility and fallback capture path documented.
• State machine, idempotency, retry and conflict behavior defined.
• Evidence/provenance and user correction behavior defined for machine-extracted facts.
• Notification/briefing behavior and suppression rules defined.
• Analytics/quality events defined without unnecessary private payloads.
• Entitlement/quota/cost behavior defined.
• Support/admin diagnostics defined without default raw-content access.
• Threat/abuse/prompt-injection implications reviewed.
• Accessibility, localization, timezone and offline impacts reviewed.
• Unit/integration/E2E/contract tests complete and observability added.
• Feature flag/kill switch/rollback strategy exists for risk level.
• User-facing privacy/help text is present before release.

Appendix J — Master Product Standard
The bar  Life Inbox succeeds when connecting the places where life already happens immediately reduces mental load. The user should feel that important information no longer disappears into email, screenshots, statements, calendars, receipts, documents and memory—and that the app can always show where its answer came from.

• If a feature increases automation but decreases trust, do not ship it yet.
• If a feature requires repeated manual organization before it becomes useful, redesign the default path.
• If an important fact cannot show evidence, treat it as a suggestion requiring review.
• If a provider cannot integrate directly, preserve value through universal capture, email/document evidence, standards, aggregators or user-authorized sharing.
• If a feature can be implemented as a reusable connector/domain/rule/permission extension rather than a special-case branch, implement the reusable foundation.
• Life Inbox should become more valuable with every month of trustworthy history—not more cluttered.
• No static specification can predict every future provider or life situation; the completeness strategy is an extensible architecture with explicit contracts for new objects, sources, permissions, rules, actions and entitlements.
